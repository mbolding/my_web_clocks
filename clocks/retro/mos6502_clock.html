The 6502 ran the Apple II, the Commodore 64, the NES and the Atari 2600. This one is emulated in the page, along with a small assembler that turned the source into the bytes in the listing: address, machine code, then the line it came from.

        
At reset the program reads the time once from a clock chip at $D000. After that the CPU keeps it alone. Once a second an interrupt arrives; the handler sets decimal mode (SED), so adding 1 to $59 gives $60, not $5A, and carries seconds into minutes and minutes into hours. Then it splits each byte into two digits and looks up their segments in a table. The rest of the second the CPU spins in IDLE, a million cycles a second, as a real one at 1 MHz would.

        
Press edit to change the program and assemble it. Try deleting the LDA TIMER that acknowledges the interrupt, and watch the clock race. Reload to start over.
