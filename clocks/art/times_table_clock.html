Times table clock

multiplication on a circle, in the clock's own arithmetic

A clock face already does arithmetic that wraps round. Five hours after nine o'clock is two o'clock, because 9 + 5 = 14, and 14 goes once round the twelve hours with 2 left over. Mathematicians write this 14 ≡ 2 (mod 12), and call it arithmetic modulo 12.

This clock does the same with multiplication. Put N points evenly round a circle, numbered 0 to N − 1 clockwise from the top, and draw a straight line from every point k to the point k × m, counting on round the circle and wrapping past N as often as it takes. That is the m times table, modulo N:

k → k × m (mod N) 7 × 2 = 14 ≡ 2 (mod 12)

With enough points the lines crowd together along curves that no single line draws, their envelope. The 2 times table wraps a cardioid, a heart shape with one cusp. The 3 times table wraps a nephroid, a kidney shape with two. For any whole number m the envelope is an epicycloid, the curve traced by a point on a small wheel rolling round the outside of a fixed one, and it has m − 1 cusps. When m is not a whole number the lines no longer close up neatly at point 0, and the figure is caught halfway as one curve melts into the next.

How the time drives it

m = hour + 1 + minutes / 60

The hour runs from 1 to 12, so at one o'clock the multiplier is 2 and the chords wrap a cardioid; at two it is 3, a nephroid; at half past three it is 4.5, on its way from three cusps to four. Count the cusps and you have the hour; see how far the figure has melted towards the next and you have the minutes, roughly. At twelve the multiplier reaches 13, and twelve cusps; at one o'clock it falls back to 2. The extra 1 is there because the 1 times table joins every point to itself, so the dial would stand empty for a whole hour. The seconds nudge m on too, so the figure is always moving, very slowly.

The second hand points at a point k, and the white line is its chord, from k to k × m; once a minute it sweeps out the whole figure. Choose 12 or 60 points to see the single chords: with 12, the points are the hour marks and the arithmetic is modulo 12, like the clock's own. Hover over the dial, or tap it, to follow any point's chord, or move the slider to try a multiplier of your own.
